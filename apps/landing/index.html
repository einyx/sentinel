What it does

Finds them

Every commit, every branch, every file. The scanner walks the whole history of a repository, not just the newest commit, so a secret that was pushed a year ago and force-pushed away is still found.

Knows whether they work

A revoked credential and a live one are different problems. Sentinel asks the provider, so the console can tell you which secrets still work and which are noise.

Keeps track of them

Every finding has a status, an owner and a note. A secret that was dealt with stays dealt with, so it does not come back as new noise on every scan.

Stops them at the door

A workflow scans every push before it is merged. The same scanner runs in the pipeline, on your machine, and on the schedule — so a finding means the same thing everywhere.

Stays out of the way

Secrets are encrypted where they are stored, masked in every report, and never handed out. The scanner's own history is the only place they are kept in the clear, and it does not survive the scan.

Is yours

It runs on your infrastructure and talks to your forge. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing that leaves the building — not the repositories, not the secrets, not the audit log.

How it works

  1. 01

    Register a repository

    Its clone URL and a read-only token. The token is encrypted before it is stored, and it is handed to git in a way that keeps it out of every URL and every file.

  2. 02

    The worker scans it

    It clones the repository, walks the history, and reports what it finds. Deleted branches are kept, because that is where a secret stays hidden.

  3. 03

    You deal with what matters

    The console shows the live ones first. Revoke the credential, mark the finding resolved, and it will not be reported again.
